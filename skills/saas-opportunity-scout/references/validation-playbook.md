# Customer Validation Playbook

## Objective

Reduce the largest commercial uncertainty before committing to substantial product development.

## 1. Problem interviews

Recruit 10–20 people matching the proposed buyer/user profile where practical. A small sample is directional, not statistically representative.

Ask about past behavior:
1. Walk me through the last time this problem happened.
2. How do you handle it today?
3. How often does it happen?
4. How much time, money or risk does it create?
5. What have you already tried?
6. Who owns the budget or approves a purchase?
7. What happens if nothing changes?

Avoid leading questions and pitching too early. Record consent and do not invent interview results.

## 2. Existing spend

Look for:
- Current software subscriptions.
- Staff time and manual work.
- Outsourced services.
- Consultants and agencies.
- Error, delay or compliance costs.

Existing spend is a useful signal, but it does not automatically transfer to a new vendor.

## 3. Solution tests

Choose the smallest test that can reveal whether the value proposition resonates:
- Landing page with a clear target segment and CTA.
- Clickable prototype.
- Concierge/manual service.
- Demo using realistic sample data.
- Technical proof of concept.
- Paid pilot proposal.

Do not build a complete platform to test a single assumption.

## 4. Payment evidence ladder

From weaker to stronger:
1. Positive opinion.
2. Stated interest.
3. Qualified request for more information.
4. Introduction to budget owner.
5. Procurement or security review.
6. Written pilot commitment.
7. Paid pilot or pre-sale.
8. Renewal and repeat usage.

Do not report these signals as equivalent.

## 5. Distribution experiments

Test plausible channels:
- Founder-led outbound.
- Industry partnerships.
- Professional associations and communities.
- Search/content.
- Integrations and marketplaces.
- Resellers and consultants.
- Industry events.

Track response, qualified meetings, conversion, time and cost. Do not claim CAC is validated until enough real acquisition data exists.

## 6. Retention tests

Measure:
- Activation and time-to-value.
- Frequency of core workflow usage.
- Repeat usage.
- Customer-reported outcomes.
- Renewal and churn.
- Expansion and referrals.

A product that is tried once but not retained may not support recurring revenue.

## Experiment card

For every experiment define:
- Hypothesis:
- Target participant:
- Method:
- Sample and recruitment:
- Budget:
- Duration:
- Success threshold:
- Failure threshold:
- Observed result:
- Interpretation:
- Decision / next step:

Define thresholds before running the experiment.

## Decision

- BUILD: evidence supports a controlled MVP.
- VALIDATE: interest exists but buyer/payment/channel assumptions remain open.
- PIVOT: problem is real but proposed solution or positioning fails.
- REJECT: pain is weak or economics/distribution are implausible.

Never fabricate interviews, conversion rates, commitments, retention or revenue.
