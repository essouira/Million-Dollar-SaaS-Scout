# Market Intelligence Playbook

## Objective

Find markets where a specific customer segment has a recurring, costly problem and current alternatives leave a meaningful gap.

## Market map

For each market, identify:
- Value chain and major participants.
- Customer segments and company sizes.
- Users, buyers, budget owners and influencers.
- Core workflows and systems of record.
- Existing vendors and substitutes.
- Regulatory or operational constraints.
- Digital maturity and purchasing behavior.

## Discovery signals

Investigate:
- Repeated manual work and spreadsheet-based operations.
- Complaints about cost, complexity, reliability or support.
- Disconnected systems and missing integrations.
- Job postings that reveal recurring operational tasks.
- RFPs, procurement notices and compliance requirements.
- Public product reviews and professional communities.
- Industry-specific forums and user groups.
- Evidence of existing spend on consultants, staff or software.

Treat each signal as a hypothesis to verify, not as proof of demand.

## Competitive landscape

Classify:
- Direct competitors: same buyer and problem.
- Indirect competitors: different product, same job to be done.
- Substitutes: spreadsheets, agencies, consultants or internal tools.
- Open-source alternatives.
- Incumbent platforms that could add the feature.

For each meaningful alternative, investigate positioning, target segment, capabilities, integrations, pricing, distribution, customer feedback and switching friction.

## Market sizing

Prefer bottom-up estimates.

- TAM: all plausible customers × annual revenue per customer.
- SAM: customers reachable with the product, geography and business model.
- SOM: a defensible obtainable share over a stated period.

State the source and assumptions for each input. Avoid unsupported top-down market-size claims. A large TAM does not prove a startup can win.

## Research quality

For material claims:
- Link the source.
- Record its publication/access date where possible.
- Prefer primary sources.
- Seek independent corroboration.
- Distinguish fact, estimate, inference and unknown.
- Note geographic and segment limitations.

Never invent interviews, customer counts, market growth, prices or competitor weaknesses.

## Deliverable

Summarize:
1. Market structure.
2. Target segments.
3. Pain points and evidence.
4. Existing alternatives.
5. Competitive gaps.
6. Bottom-up market assumptions.
7. Risks and unanswered questions.
8. Best next customer-discovery experiment.
